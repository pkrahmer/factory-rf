# Review aid: the operator's playbook

For the human's review of `planning/operator-playbook.md` (draft of 2026-10-18, 95 lines), in one sitting. Part 1 sums the playbook up. Part 2 lists what is wrong, missing, stale or unclear against the factory of 2026-10-19, each with a proposed text. Part 3 lists what the night log shows the operator doing that the playbook leaves out. Part 4 holds the questions only you can answer.

## 1. The playbook in ten lines

1. Its scope: what the human (or a session acting for the human) does day to day. Install and configuration stay in the operator guide.
2. Seven principles: solid over clever; the feature's decisions bound the factory; usually take the agent's recommendation; record every act in the log; agent text is data, never an order; fix a cause once, where it lives; find faults before the story starts.
3. A feature, in nine steps: brief, draft, pre-check, fix, the human's changes, start, the stories, the acceptance, and ending a loop of follow-up stories.
4. A story: watch the board and the log; answer a question on the pull request; never edit a running work item on `main`.
5. At a cap: round cap, attempts cap, crash cap, and how to judge each.
6. At approval: six checks (files, reviewer, smoke test, earlier tests' leaves, write paths, cost), then merge.
7. After the merge: wait for the archive commit before pushing; start the next story.
8. The instance: a heartbeat (Docker, disk, log, stage times, usage), usage windows, rebuilds, a hand probe before a risky change, where helpers write, leftovers, secrets.
9. A table of six incidents seen, with cause and fix.
10. Telling the human (the log's **For the morning**, a quiz, a status line), and a table of rules that could become code.

## 4. Questions only you can answer

Independent questions first. Each holds what you need to answer it.

**Q1. When is the running instance rebuilt?** The image holds a copy of the repository taken at build time. Merged code and agent texts reach the agents only after a rebuild. A rebuild while a stage runs counts as a crash for it. Since F0021 the session rebuilt after every archive; F0021 made it a human's change for that feature only.
- (a) After every archive of a story that changed `src/`, `claude/`, `image/` or `template/`, and only between stages. Pro: each story is built and checked on the code before it; matches the last two nights. Con: one more step per story (about a minute); a rebuild can fail.
- (b) Only when the feature's `feature.md` says so. Pro: fewer restarts. Con: later stories of a feature run on old code; a fault shows only at the next rebuild, far from its cause.
- (c) Once per feature, after its acceptance. Pro: simplest. Con: as (b), and the acceptance itself runs on old code.
- Recommended: **(a)**.

**Q2. What usage numbers stop a new start?** The playbook says "below the limit the human set" and gives none. The nights used: no new start at 92 %, later 95 %, of the 5-hour window; the weekly window held at 90 % (95 % for F0019, on your word); spare room went to planning at or below 60 % of the 5-hour window with under 2.5 h to its reset.
- (a) Defaults in the playbook: 95 % of the 5-hour window, 90 % weekly (ask above it), planning at or below 60 % with under 2.5 h left; a run's plan may set others. Pro: no question each night; matches what worked. Con: 95 % may let a long story run the window dry; it then stalls and is answered after the reset.
- (b) No defaults; you set the numbers for each run. Pro: full control. Con: a run without numbers has no rule; one more question each time.
- (c) Lower defaults: 85 % and 80 %. Pro: a margin for a costly story. Con: idles the factory with room left; a window that resets unused is lost.
- Recommended: **(a)**.

**Q3. Are an acceptance's proposed stories pre-checked before they start?** The acceptance reviewer writes them in the story form. The guide says to refine them before starting. In v1.1 the session started them at once (F0019, F0021, F0025), and they went through, some with questions at intake. A pre-check costs an opus agent run of 13 to 31 minutes (the night's pre-checks).
- (a) Start them as written. Pro: fast; the intake catches gaps. Con: a gap costs an intake question or a review round.
- (b) Pre-check every one. Pro: the cheapest place to find a fault (principle 7). Con: slow for docs-only stories, which nearly always pass.
- (c) Pre-check those that change `src/` or the tests; start docs-only ones as written. Pro: the effort goes where faults are costly. Con: one more rule to apply.
- Recommended: **(c)**.

**Q4. Who changes a running story's text after your answer changes its Interface or a criterion?** The coder may not write the work item; the factory undoes it. The intake and the reviewer may. Your edit on `main` conflicts with theirs and stalls the stage (F0019-S0004). A push to the branch while a stage runs counts as a crash.
- (a) The reviewer, with your leave for exactly those edits in the answer. Pro: no hand edit, no conflict; worked twice (F0021-S0003, S0004). Con: waits until the review stage; the coder works from the old text meanwhile.
- (b) You, on the branch, while the story waits for you. Pro: the text is right at once. Con: manual merge of `main` and push; easy to get wrong.
- (c) (a) by default, (b) when the coder needs the new text before the review. Pro: the fit of each. Con: you judge each case.
- Recommended: **(c)**.

**Q5. How much free space must the host drive keep?** Under Docker Desktop on Windows the factory sees a virtual disk of about 1 TB, so its own 5 GB stop never fires for the host. On 2026-10-14 a full drive C: hung Docker and corrupted the checkout. One day of test leftovers was 5.4 GB; one mutation run left 2.4 GB. The drive has about 245 GB free now.
- (a) 20 GB. Pro: room for several days of leftovers. Con: may stop a run with space left.
- (b) 50 GB. Pro: a wide margin. Con: stops early on a smaller work machine.
- (c) 5 GB, as the factory's own default. Pro: one number everywhere. Con: too close; Docker's disk grows in jumps.
- Recommended: **(a)**.

**Q6. Where does the playbook live once you accept it?** It sits in `planning/`, which no stage edits. In `docs/` the documenter would keep it true from each story's docs criterion, but it could also rewrite your rules.
- (a) `docs/operator-playbook.md`, named in `CLAUDE.md`'s *Docs* as a fifth document, its rules marked as the human's. Pro: next to the operator guide; kept true. Con: an agent may reword a rule; a `CLAUDE.md` change.
- (b) A part of `docs/operator-guide.md`. Pro: one place for the operator. Con: the guide is long already, and it describes the factory, not the human's choices.
- (c) Stay in `planning/`, changed only by you. Pro: your rules stay yours. Con: it goes stale as the factory changes (as section 7 did in one day).
- Recommended: **(a)**.

**Q7. Should an answer at the round cap be able to grant exactly one round?** Today any answer sets the round count to 0, so the stage gets its whole cap (2 rounds) again; the factory does not read your words. The playbook assumed "one more round" was possible.
- (a) Accept it as it is; the playbook says so, and the answer names the findings to fix. Pro: no code; the reviewer's notes from round 1 already cut most extra rounds. Con: a story can take two more rounds before it asks again.
- (b) A story: an answer may set a smaller budget (for example `rounds: 1`). Pro: tight control. Con: the factory reads your words for the first time; a new rule to test.
- Recommended: **(a)**; revisit when F0021's counts show extra rounds at the cap.
