## Points for the human

1. **How `ignored` asks git, since `-z` needs `--stdin` and `run_process` feeds no input.**
   - A (recommended, edits 1 and 2): the argument form `git check-ignore --verbose --non-matching -- <paths...>`, one line per path in order, read by position; groups of at most 1,000 paths per call, so that a run that left tens of thousands of cache files on the dataset never meets the argument-length limit (3,000 paths were 170 KB; Linux allows about 2 MB).
   - B: `git check-ignore -z --stdin` with the paths NUL-separated on standard input. `ProcessRequest` gains an `input: bytes | None` field and `run_process` a writer beside its selector loop (an input larger than the pipe buffer must be written while the output is read, or both sides block). A change to the one process module, pinned by several structural tests, for one caller.
   - C: A without the groups, one call however many paths; simpler, and `GitError` ("Argument list too long") on a pathological run.
2. **A symbolic link at the target** (edit 5): the dataset's changed file replaces an entry on the host that may be a symbolic link an earlier take brought in; a plain write follows the link and changes its target, which may lie outside the checkout. A: say in the Interface that the existing entry is removed first and test it (recommended; two rows). B: leave it to the coder and the reviewer.
3. **Where the shared rules live** (edit 4): A (recommended): they stay in `factory.application.undo` and become public, so the take imports from the undo; F0044's story 5 (the undo goes) moves them into the take when `UndoService` goes. B: they move into `factory.application.take` now and the undo imports from the take: the old module depends on the new one, and `tests/application/test_undo.py` still passes.
