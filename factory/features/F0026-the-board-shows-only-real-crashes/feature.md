# The board shows only real crashes

## Goal

On the night of 2026-10-19 `factory-watch` showed `event: crash:<id>` for `F0021-A0001`, `A0002`, `A0003` and `F0025-A0001` while each ran, though no crash happened: the journals hold `crashes: 0` and the factory log no `iteration failed`. The cause is not the acceptances: it is how `factory-watch` judges a claim of its own instance. `factory-watch` evaluates "as a tick would" (`Watcher(boundaries, instance).evaluate`) and runs as the container's instance (`self`), so a claim of that instance is `own` (`claim_status` in `factory.domain.lease`: "own" whatever its age), and the evaluation maps `own` to `crash` (`CLAIM_EVENTS` in `factory.domain.evaluation`). For the tick that is right: one process, one tick at a time, so when the tick looks, no stage of its own is running and an own claim is a stage that died. `factory-watch` is a second process that looks while the tick runs a stage, so the claim of the running stage reads as a crash. Every stage the instance runs shows it, stories as much as acceptances (S0001's smoke test shows it for a story claimed one minute before); the night's looks at 02:16 and 03:57 fell on acceptances, whose reviewer runs up to 90 minutes. This feature lets `factory-watch` judge its own instance's claim by the claim's lease, as it already judges another instance's: within the lease the stage may be running, and the line is `event: idle:-  <work item> is held by <instance>`; at and past the lease a live stage can hold it only for the factory's own steps around its run and checks, at most about a minute (a role's `time_limit` and its stage's checks and reports fit within its `lease_duration`), so the line is `event: crash:<work item>`. The tick's judgement does not change: it still reruns its own claim at once after a restart. It is for the operator who reads the board at night. The brief's second fault, the flaky `tests/application/test_watcher_move_rule.py::test_every_factory_move_is_no_reject[tests-doing]`, gets no story: no cause was found (decision 6), so there is nothing to fix that would not be a guess; the human's change 1 makes its next failure leave the failure text in the journal. Done when every story below is merged and `make check` is green.

## Decisions taken for the human

1. **The cause of the false crash** is the second process, not the acceptance. `factory-watch` runs `Watcher(boundaries, instance).evaluate`, the tick's own evaluation, as the container's instance. `claim_status` gives `own` for a claim of that instance "whatever its age, so a restart under the same name reruns at once", and `CLAIM_EVENTS` maps `own` to `crash`. While the tick runs a stage, the stage's pushed claim is on the remote-tracking ref of that very checkout, so every look during a stage, story or acceptance, shows `crash:<id>`. Checked on 2026-10-19 in a throwaway container of `factory-self-factory` on a copy of the checkout: a story whose branch holds a claim of `self` started 1 minute before gives `event: crash:F0001-S0001` (S0001's smoke test). The suggested causes do not hold: an acceptance's front matter has every field a story's state has (`AcceptanceState` derives from `WorkItemState`), and no run record is read by the evaluation. (Feature)
2. **From outside, an own claim is judged by its lease** (`observed_claim_status`): `held` while the claim is younger than its `lease_duration`, `own` (a crash) at and past it, every other claim as `claim_status` judges it. So the line is `event: idle:-  F0021-A0001 is held by self` while the acceptance runs, the text the board already gives another instance's live claim. The tick keeps `claim_status`: it never looks while its own stage runs, and a restarted instance must rerun its dead stage at once, not after the lease (R20). The two judgements differ only in an own claim within its lease. The choice is a keyword of `Watcher`, `observer`, `False` for the tick and `True` for `factory-watch`; the evaluation is not changed, since it takes the claim status as a function already. (S0001)
3. **A real crash still shows**, at the latest when its lease runs out. A live stage that uses every limit can show as `crash:<id>` for up to about a minute past its lease (the agent run's 30-second grace, and the factory's steps before the run and after the checks); that is a display only, and the same margin another instance's `expired` already has. Within the lease an own crash is handled by the tick at its first look after the restart (the container's `restart: unless-stopped`), so `factory-watch` would show it only in that window. A crash the tick does not reach, because a stop is in force or the process is down while the container runs, shows as `crash:<id>` once the lease has run out. Chosen over a sign of life of the tick (Out of scope): that would be a new mechanism for a window of one tick. Chosen over showing an own claim always as `held`, which would hide a crash forever. (S0001)
4. **Where F0026 touches F0020.** After F0020-S0007 a stage that waits out a provider limit holds its claim past its `lease_duration` (F0020's decision 14). During such a wait, past the lease, `factory-watch` shows `crash:<id>` although the stage waits; the factory log's line of the wait tells the two apart, and the tick does not act on it (an own claim is handled only at its first look after a restart). Accepted: a wait that long is rare, and anything better needs the sign of life of decision 3. The operator guide says so. S0001 touches `watcher.py`, `watch.py`, `claims.py` and `lease.py`, none of which F0020, F0022, F0023 or F0024 change. S0001 starts after F0020-S0007 is archived: its docs criterion describes the provider-limit wait that S0007 builds. (S0001)

Its two tests keep their names and what they check. No other earlier test changes: the tick's rows of `tests/application/test_watcher.py::test_the_claim_tier_is_decided_by_the_claim_service` keep their results, and `tests/entry/test_watch.py::test_run_watch_writes_the_event_a_blank_line_and_the_board` compares with a `Watcher` on a remote without a claim, where both judgements agree. (S0001)
6. **The flaky test: no cause found, so no fix and no leave.** `test_every_factory_move_is_no_reject[tests-doing]` failed once under the parallel `make check` of F0021-S0004 (journal entry 10) and passed three times alone; its failure text was not recorded, and the JUnit report of that run is gone. Read against the four suggested causes: it waits on no time limit (the only one on its path is git's 5 minutes, `GIT_TIME_LIMIT`); it shares no state (its remote is a per-test copy of a per-worker template, its clones lie in `tmp_path`, `FIXTURE` is a frozen model, the fake hosting service and the clock are per test); every git call runs without the user's or the system's configuration (`GIT_CONFIG_GLOBAL` is `/dev/null`), and no file lies outside `tmp_path`; and nothing depends on order (each row builds its remote from the template, and `factory_moves` gives the same rows on every worker). The commits it makes form one line, which `--date-order` lists child first whatever their timestamps. Ten full parallel runs (20 and 40 workers, with coverage) and 30 runs of its module beside `tests/application/test_watcher.py` on 20 workers under 40 busy loops, in a throwaway container of `factory-self-factory` on 2026-10-19, passed every time. A change to the test now would be a guess at the symptom. So the test stays as it is, and the human's change 1 makes the next failure leave its text in the journal; that text names the cause, and a story then fixes it. (Feature)
